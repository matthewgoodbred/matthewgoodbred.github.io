About

I am a Ph.D. student in the Department of Astrophysical Sciences at Princeton University, where I work with Anatoly Spitkovsky on the plasma physics of compact objects. My work is largely computational: I run large three-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations to follow the behavior of relativistic plasmas from the scale of individual particle orbits up to the global structure of a neutron star's magnetosphere.

Right now I am most interested in what happens inside the closed zone of a pulsar magnetosphere — how the trapped charge settles into a disk-dome equilibrium, how the diocotron instability breaks that equilibrium, and how the resulting slow transport of charge across field lines might show up in the radio emission we actually observe. Before Princeton I worked on the theory of relativistic magnetic reconnection, which remains a central thread in my research.

I did my undergraduate work at Dartmouth College, graduating with high honors in physics in 2023, where I worked with Yi-Hsin Liu on the first-principles theory of the reconnection rate. You can read more on the research and publications pages.

Away from the desk

I hike and backpack whenever I can get away for long enough to make it worthwhile, and I have a particular weakness for long routes above treeline. I also take a camera along, which means I come back with far more photographs of mountains than anyone needs.
