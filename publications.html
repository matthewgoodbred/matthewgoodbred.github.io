Diocotron Modes in Pulsar Magnetospheres: Charge Diffusion and Implications for Radio Emission Variability

M. Goodbred & A. Spitkovsky · The Astrophysical Journal, accepted (2026)

arXiv:2606.03984

The closed zone of a pulsar magnetosphere is usually treated as inert: charge settles into a disk-dome equilibrium and stays there. Using 3D particle-in-cell simulations of aligned and oblique rotators, we show that this equilibrium is unstable to the diocotron mode, and that it saturates into a long-lived m = 1 pattern carrying a few percent of the central point charge.

We derive a quasilinear radial diffusion coefficient for the resulting stochastic transport of charge across field lines, and use a multipole expansion to translate the diocotron charge moments into perturbations of the polar-cap potential and the emission beam. The predicted modulation timescales and amplitudes are in the range associated with nulling, periodic amplitude modulation, and drifting subpulses — suggesting that some radio variability is set in the closed zone rather than at the polar cap.

First-Principles Theory of the Relativistic Magnetic Reconnection Rate in Astrophysical Pair Plasmas

M. Goodbred & Y.-H. Liu · Physical Review Letters 129, 265101 (2022)

Journal arXiv

Reconnection in the magnetically dominated pair plasmas around compact objects proceeds at a rate of roughly a tenth of the Alfvén speed, a value that simulations reproduce but that no theory predicted from first principles.

We show that the rate is fixed by the geometry of the diffusion region itself: the pressure of the reconnected field pushing back on the upstream plasma limits how open the inflow region can become, and that constraint alone sets the rate without any free parameter. The prediction matches large-scale particle-in-cell simulations across the magnetization range relevant to magnetars, pulsar winds, and black hole coronae.

The relation between the energy conversion rate and reconnection rate in Petschek-type reconnection — Implications for solar flares

M. Goodbred, Y.-H. Liu, B. Chen & X. Li · Physics of Plasmas 28, 082103 (2021)

arXiv

Solar flare observations measure how quickly magnetic energy is converted into plasma energy, but the quantity theory predicts is the reconnection rate — the speed at which field lines are processed through the diffusion region. The two are related, but not identical.

We derive the relation between them for Petschek-type reconnection and show how the ratio depends on the opening angle of the outflow exhaust. This gives a route from a measured flare energy budget back to the underlying reconnection geometry.
