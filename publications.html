Diocotron Modes in Pulsar Magnetospheres: Charge Diffusion and Implications for Radio Emission Variability

M. Goodbred & A. Spitkovsky · The Astrophysical Journal, accepted (2026)

arXiv:2606.03984

The closed field line region of a pulsar magnetosphere is usually treated as inert and filled with charge. However, weak pulsars should have regions in their magnetospheres where large zones of vacuum live in between charge-separated plasma. The charge between these vacuum 'gaps' does not rotate with the star, rendering it susceptible to the diocotron instability. Commonly encountered in non-neutral laboratory plasmas, the diocotron instability leads to the formation of charge vortices and non-axisymmetric charge distributions. Using 3D particle-in-cell simulations, we show that the instability in the equatorial disk of a pulsar saturates into a long-lived m = 1 pattern.

Azimuthal electric fields from the diocotron mode kick charges radially across field lines, leading to efficient diffusion of charges towards the outer edge of the closed zone. The result is a persistent, non-axisymmetric 'blob', spanning much of the closed magnetosphere, which super-rotates with respect to the star. The corresponding electric fields can exert time dependent perturbations on the polar cap where the radio emission is generated. Deflection of the emission beam and modulation of the polar cap potential drop by the diocotron mode might be related to variability in pulsar radio emission, such as nulling and periodic amplitude modulation.

First-Principles Theory of the Relativistic Magnetic Reconnection Rate in Astrophysical Pair Plasmas

M. Goodbred & Y.-H. Liu · Physical Review Letters 129, 265101 (2022)

Journal arXiv:2301.12111

Magnetic reconnection is often invoked to explain rapid release of magnetic energy in astrophysical systems, from solar flares to magnetar bursts. To explain observations, however, reconnection must proceed at a rate order of magnitude faster than classical theory predicts. We develop a theory that can explain the fast reconnection rates in relativistic pair plasmas based on first-principles kinetic physics.

As plasma flows into the reconnection region, is it accelerated in order to carry the currents which support the magnetic field reversal. When the background magnetic field dominates the rest mass energy density of the plasma, particles accelerated in the sheet attain enormous energies, but little energy is left to enhance the thermal pressure of the plasma. The upstream magnetic field collapses inwards, creating a compact diffusion region with fast inflow and outflow. Quantitative predictions of the pressure at the reconnection site and the resulting fast reconnection rate are in excellent agreement with particle-in-cell simulations of relativistic reconnection.

The relation between the energy conversion rate and reconnection rate in Petschek-type reconnection — Implications for solar flares

M. Goodbred, Y.-H. Liu, B. Chen & X. Li · Physics of Plasmas 28, 082103 (2021)

Journal

In the Petschek model of magnetic reconnection, energy conversion occurs in shocks which bound the outflow exhaust. We solve for the energization of the plasma across the shock structure using a Riemann solver across different parameter regimes and compare it to the energy conversion in particle-in-cell simulations. The energy conversion rates agree reasonably well, but the shock structure cannot capture magnetic islands, which are important sites of energy conversion in reconnection.
